== Abstract

AmiSpec is an open standard for declarative description of infrastructure topologies and software component passports. A *ComponentPassport* specifies a reusable class of services (availability modes, resource formulas, client sidecars, storage, backups, day-2 actions). A *TopologyBlueprint* specifies a concrete deployment (clusters, nodes, component instances, bindings, external resources). The pair compiles deterministically into a full declarative manifest tree for Kubernetes and GitOps.

This document is both the normative specification of AmiSpec schemas and the layered user guide (getting started → practical patterns → reference). It is published under the Apache-2.0 license and intended for public use.

== Scope
[[scope]]

This document specifies the *AmiSpec* standard for describing:

. *Component passports* (`ComponentPassport`, `catalog.amispec.io/v1`) — reusable descriptions of service classes: availability modes, workload engines, resource and kernel formulas, network slots, client-sidecar contracts (`clientContract`), storage, backup, live migration, observability and day-2 actions.
. *Topology blueprints* (`TopologyBlueprint`, `topology.amispec.io/v1`) — concrete deployments: clusters, nodes, load balancers, security zones, component instances, bindings, external resources, multi-cluster links.
. *Compilation model* — deterministic projection from the pair (passport + topology) into a staged declarative manifest tree (Kubernetes + GitOps), including provenance metadata for reverse decompilation.

*In scope:* schema structure and field semantics; typologies of availability modes; binding and client-connection contracts; resource/disk/kernel arbitration rules; multi-cluster relations; security zones; modular project layout; formula language usage (CEL); conformance criteria for passports, topologies, and compilers.

*Out of scope:* concrete product UIs, commercial hosting offerings, specific cloud-provider billing, and any vendor-portal implementation. Reference tooling names (`ami compile`, `ami decompile`, `ami import-k8s`) illustrate the model and are not themselves normative API surface beyond the data contracts defined here.

== Terms and Definitions
[[terms]]

For the purposes of this document, the following terms and definitions apply. Definitions are declarative and do not themselves contain requirements.

[cols="2,5", options="header"]
|===
| Term | Definition

| *AmiSpec*
| The open standard defined by this document for declarative description of component passports and infrastructure topologies.

| *ComponentPassport*
| A reusable description of a class of services, including availability modes, workload, interfaces, hardware and operations (API version `catalog.amispec.io/v1`).

| *TopologyBlueprint*
| A concrete deployment description: clusters, nodes, component instances, bindings and external resources (API version `topology.amispec.io/v1`).

| *Passport revision* (`passportRev`)
| Version of the passport *rules* (formulas, sidecars, quotas), independent of the software `version` of the component.

| *Slot*
| Named outbound capability of a provider (`provides[].slot`).

| *Requirement*
| Named inbound need of a consumer (`requires[<name>]`) and the mapping of exported tokens to environment or files.

| *Binding*
| Directed graph edge from a consumer to a provider slot, optionally carrying `envPrefix`, rotation epoch and delivery guarantees.

| *clientContract*
| Contract by which the *provider* passport dictates the local proxy sidecar injected into the consumer pod; the application connects to `127.0.0.1`.

| *KEP-753 sidecar*
| Native Kubernetes sidecar (`initContainers` with `restartPolicy: Always`): starts before the app, stops after it, terminates in Jobs.

| *KEP-1287*
| In-place update of Pod resources (cgroup v2) without container restart.

| *Capacity model*
| Component that validates node budgets (RAM, disk, isolation, surge envelope) before deploy.

| *Node Readiness Gate*
| Node label affirming that a kernel/system tuning profile has been applied successfully.

| *Monotonic merge*
| Algebraic merge of `sysctl`/`sysfs` profiles via `mergeStrategy` (`max` / `min` / `exclusive-match`).

| *Mount flag (`#MountFlag`)*
| Closed set of allowed Linux/CSI mount options for `lvmOptions.mountFlags` (default `noatime`, `nodiratime`). Dangerous flags (`suid`, `exec`, `dev`, `user`) are out of the set.

| *Degraded HA*
| Two-node availability mode with witness or cloud lease fencing.

| *Expand / Contract*
| Two-phase database schema migration: additive changes before rollout, destructive changes after.

| *Transactional outbox*
| Guaranteed event delivery via an outbox table and a relay sidecar.

| *PITR*
| Point-in-Time Recovery — restoration to a specified timestamp.

| *Provenance metadata*
| Labels and annotations on generated manifests that make compilation reversible.

| *Reverse compilation*
| Folding a Provenance-carrying manifest tree back into `TopologyBlueprint` (lossless Level A) or heuristic import of foreign manifests (Level B).

| *Day-2 actions*
| Declarative service operations (`day2Actions`) with allowlisted ids and roles.

| *Fleet*
| Group of homogeneous edge clusters (`fleetCount`).

| *Cutover*
| Transactional traffic switch during migration or disaster recovery.

| *Normative*
| Content that sets requirements for conformance.

| *Informative*
| Content provided for understanding; not required for conformance.
|===

== Conformance
[[conformance]]

Conformance to AmiSpec is defined for three artifact classes.

=== Conforming passport

A ComponentPassport document conforms when:

. It validates against the CUE meta-schema `schemas/component_passport.cue` with no errors.
. `metadata.id` matches `^[a-z0-9-]+$` and `metadata.passportRev` is an integer `>= 1`.
. Every declared `topologyModes` entry provides `description`, `minNodes`, `replicas`, `scaleDownPolicy` and `disruptionBudget`.
. Every `workload.configFormulas` entry sets `cel`, `targetPath`, `applyImpact` and `description`.
. For each `provides[].clientContract.sidecar`, `sharedQuotaFormula` (when present) is a valid CEL expression over the documented context; listeners and `exportedTokens` are consistent with `requires[].envMapping` of typical consumers.
. Operations that claim backup or migration capability fill the corresponding `operations.backup` / `operations.liveMigration` blocks completely.

=== Conforming topology

A TopologyBlueprint document conforms when:

. It validates against `schemas/topology_spec.cue` with no errors.
. Every `clusters[].cni.clusterMeshId` is unique within the blueprint and in `1..50`; induced Pod and Service CIDRs do not collide with existing networks.
. Every `clusters[].security.sopsRecipients` is non-empty and well-formed.
. Component instances pin `passport`, `version` and `passportRev`; `config` keys are declared in the referenced passport `parameters` (or are explicitly reserved).
. Bindings resolve: `to` exists, `toSlot`/`asRequirement` match provider/consumer declarations, and `deliveryGuarantee: transactional-outbox` sets `outboxSourceRequirement`.
. Node inventory is consistent with `minNodes` of selected `topologyModes`; disk allocations respect system and snapshot reserves.

=== Conforming compiler / toolchain

An implementation of the AmiSpec compilation model conforms when:

. Compilation is deterministic: identical (passport set + topology) yields identical manifest tree (up to allowed timestamp fields).
. All *compiler invariants* in <<compiler-invariants>> are enforced.
. Generated manifests carry the Provenance metadata listed in this document so that lossless decompilation is possible.
. The implementation does not execute runtime `scripts` on the compile host; they are packaged content-addressed (`-<sha8>`) and mounted read-only.

=== Conformance levels

[cols="2,4", options="header"]
|===
| Level | Meaning

| *Passport-author conformant*
| Passports validate and fill required blocks; examples in this guide are followed.

| *Topology-author conformant*
| Blueprints validate; bindings, budgets and CIDR rules hold.

| *Toolchain conformant*
| Compiler and solver enforce invariants and provenance; reverse builder satisfies <<reverse-compilation>> (Level A round-trip).

| *Fully conformant ecosystem*
| All of the above plus the twelve invariants of <<invariants>> hold in running deployments.
|===

Validation matrix of industrial scenarios (see <<validation-matrix>>) serves as a recommended conformance test suite. A claim of the form “implementation X supports AmiSpec 1.0” SHOULD cite the levels above.

== Why AmiSpec

[NOTE]
====
This section is *informative*.
====

Modern Kubernetes clusters are described by dozens of disconnected manifests: Helm charts, operator CRDs, NetworkPolicy, HPA, backup jobs, migration scripts. Humans cannot see the system as a whole; automation cannot check consistency *before* deploy.

AmiSpec addresses this with two linked artifacts:

* *Component passport* (`ComponentPassport`) — a reusable description of “how this class of services works”: availability modes, tuning formulas, connection sidecars, volumes, backups, service actions. A passport is like a *class* in OOP.
* *Topology* (`TopologyBlueprint`, usually `topology.yaml`) — a concrete deployment: which instances are placed, on which nodes, with which resources and bindings. A topology is like an *instance*.

From the pair “passport + topology”, a full tree of declarative cluster manifests (Kubernetes + GitOps) is compiled deterministically. The same model of infrastructure relationships serves capacity validation, policy checks, and compilation — tooling works from one contract so that services, stores, networks, and nodes can be governed as a connected system rather than as isolated manifests.

.AmiSpec end-to-end data path
[mermaid]
----
flowchart LR
    Catalog["Passport catalog<br/>ComponentPassport"] --> Model["Infrastructure relationship model<br/>+ capacity validation"]
    Catalog --> Compiler["Compiler<br/>ami compile"]
    Model -->|"topology.yaml"| Compiler
    Compiler --> Flux["Manifest tree<br/>GitOps / FluxCD"]
----

=== Twelve invariants of the standard
[[invariants]]

The twelve base invariants are *normative*. Any conforming AmiSpec implementation and any conforming passport deployment MUST satisfy them. The invariants also explain *why* schema fields are shaped as they are and serve as review criteria.

. *GitOps First.* Deployments MUST NOT rely on manual cluster changes outside Git. A deploy is a deterministic compilation of `topology.yaml` and passports into manifests and a Git commit.
. *Zero-Privilege Control Plane.* The control plane MUST NOT have arbitrary shell access to nodes. Management flows only through Git commits and a local allowlist of permitted actions.
. *Inversion of the client balancer (`clientContract`).* The application MUST NOT choose how to connect to an HA database: the provider passport dictates which local proxy sidecar is injected into the consumer pod. The application always connects to `127.0.0.1`.
. *Kernel race protection (`Node Readiness Gate`).* A heavy datastore MUST NOT start on a node until the agent has applied the kernel tuning profile and affirmed readiness with the gate label.
. *Resource mathematics.* Datastore parameters (`shared_buffers`, JVM heap, and similar) MUST be computed by formulas from the allocated memory limit rather than set ad hoc.
. *Universal archetypes.* The archetypes `generic-stateless` and `generic-stateful` MUST allow packaging arbitrary (including commercial) software without changing the meta-schema.
. *Hybrid bridge and migration replicas.* A topology MUST be able to describe external resources and catch-up replicas with a safe cutover.
. *Multiple balancers.* A single cluster MUST be able to host `edge-proxy`, `l7-gateway`, and `l4-balancer` with distinct roles.
. *Mutations from inbound bindings.* Establishing a typed binding (for example CDC) MUST apply the corresponding provider mutations (for example `wal_level = logical`).
. *Observability economics.* Local anomaly detectors MUST be usable without mandatory external calls; expensive AI/event paths MUST be deduplicated.
. *Declarative service actions (`day2Actions`).* First-aid operations MUST be described in the passport with roles and cooldowns rather than run ad hoc.
. *Multi-cluster relations.* A single graph MUST be able to describe multiple clusters and links between them without overlapping Pod CIDR subnets.

=== Key concepts

Before the first example, six terms are enough.

[cols="2,5", options="header"]
|===
| Term | Essence

| *Passport (`ComponentPassport`)*
| Reusable description of a service class: workload engine, topology modes, config formulas, network slots, hardware requirements, backups, and Day-2 actions. Versioned via `passportRev`.

| *Topology (`TopologyBlueprint`)*
| Concrete deployment: clusters, nodes, component instances, bindings, external resources. Usually lives in `topology.yaml` or the modular tree `topology/`.

| *Slot (`provides[].slot`)*
| Named outbound capability of a provider: “what I can share” (SQL cluster, KV, queue, REST API).

| *Requirement (`requires[<slot>]`)*
| Named inbound need of a consumer: “what I need” and how exported values become environment variables.

| *Binding (`binding`)*
| Graph edge: a consumer connects to a provider slot. A binding may carry `envPrefix`, a secret rotation epoch, and a delivery guarantee.

| *Topology mode (`topologyModes`)*
| Availability profile: `standalone`, `degraded-ha`, `strict-ha`, `sharded-cluster` — with quorum, disruption budget, and anti-affinity.
|===

Relationships between artifacts:

[source,text]
----
Passport (class)  ──used by──►  Topology (instance)
       │                              │
       │  parameters / configFormulas │  config / resources / storage
       │  provides / requires         │  bindings / expose
       ▼                              ▼
         Deterministic compile (ami compile)
                      │
                      ▼
         Cluster declarative manifest tree
----

==== Description language

The standard is built on three layers:

* *CUE* — meta-schemas (`component_passport.cue`, `topology_spec.cue`). CUE unifies schemas and values and supports formulas and strict typing.
* *YAML* — the passports and topologies themselves: human-readable, easy to review in Git, deterministically serializable.
* *CEL (Common Expression Language)* — safe formulas for config, quotas, and policy conditions. CEL has no I/O, always terminates, and evaluates in microseconds — identically in the browser and in the compiler.

This combination is deliberate: plain JSON Schema cannot express formulas; Jsonnet/Dhall are too Turing-complete for instant bidirectional graph editing; separate runtimes (Pkl, KCL) add an unnecessary entry barrier. When needed, JSON Schema is *generated* from CUE as a machine artifact for IDEs.

=== First example: minimal topology

Below is the smallest meaningful topology: one stateless application and one database in a single-node cluster.

[source,yaml]
----
apiVersion: topology.amispec.io/v1
kind: TopologyBlueprint
metadata:
  projectId: "demo-blog"
  organization: "example-org"
  gitMode: "managed-embedded"

clusters:
  - id: local
    name: "Demo Cluster"
    domain: "example.local"
    security:
      sopsRecipients:
        - "age1examplepublickey00000000000000000000000000000000000000"
    cni:
      engine: cilium-ebpf
      clusterMeshId: 1
    nodes:
      - id: node-1
        role: control-plane-worker
        pricingTier: micro-vps
        hardware:
          arch: amd64
          cpuCores: 4
          memoryMb: 8192
          disks:
            - { name: "nvme0n1", tier: "nvme-local", sizeGb: 100 }

    components:
      - id: web
        passport: stateless-app
        version: "1.0.0"
        passportRev: 1
        workloadOverride:
          image: "ghcr.io/example/blog-web:1.0.0"
        topology: { mode: standalone, replicas: 1 }
        resources: { cpuCores: 1, memoryMb: 512 }
        expose:
          - { endpoint: web, viaBalancer: main-l7, scope: public }

      - id: db
        passport: postgresql
        version: "18.0.0"
        passportRev: 6
        topology: { mode: standalone }
        resources: { cpuCores: 2, memoryMb: 2048 }
        storage: { sizeGb: 20 }
        backupTarget: backups-s3

    bindings:
      - { from: web, to: db, toSlot: sql-cluster, asRequirement: database }

externalResources:
  - id: backups-s3
    passport: external-s3-compatible
    role: backup-vault
    endpointFqdn: "s3.example.com"
    bucket: "demo-backups"
    secretRef: "sops://secrets/s3.enc.yaml"
----

What happened here:

. One cluster `local` with a single node (memory and disk are physical hardware facts).
. Two components: a web application on the `stateless-app` archetype and PostgreSQL on the reference `postgresql` passport.
. One binding `web → db`: the compiler creates a connection secret and (in HA mode) a local `pgbouncer` sidecar in the web application pod.
. Backups go to external S3 (`externalResources`).

Fields such as `sopsRecipients`, `clusterMeshId`, and `backupTarget` are covered in Part II; at this stage it is enough to know they are required for correct compilation.

=== How a component passport is structured

A passport is a “smart service class”. The smallest meaningful passport:

[source,yaml]
----
apiVersion: catalog.amispec.io/v1
kind: ComponentPassport
metadata:
  id: demo-worker
  name: "Demo Queue Worker"
  version: "1.0.0"
  passportRev: 1
  category: application
  archetype: stateless-app
  distribution:
    tier: open-core
    license: Apache-2.0
  canvasUI:
    icon: "worker-gear"
    accentColor: "#2563EB"
    nodeShape: standard-card

topologyModes:
  standalone:
    description: "1 replica without availability guarantees"
    minNodes: 1
    replicas: 1
    scaleDownPolicy: immediate
    disruptionBudget: { maxUnavailable: 1, updateStrategy: rolling }
    podAntiAffinity: soft

workload:
  engine: k8s-deployment
  priorityClass: background-batch
  container:
    image: "ghcr.io/example/demo-worker:1.0.0"
    readOnlyRootFilesystem: true
  probes:
    liveness: { type: exec, command: ["pgrep", "-f", "worker"], localProcessOnly: true }

interfaces:
  egressDefaultMode: graph-only
  endpoints: []
  provides: []
  requires:
    database:
      capability: postgres-sql
      optional: false
      dependencyStage: infrastructure-ready
      envMapping:
        DATABASE_URL: "${exported.uri}"

hardwareAndKernel:
  resources:
    minCpuCores: 0.25
    minMemoryMb: 128
    recommendedMemoryMb: 512

operations:
  observability:
    scrapeInterval: "30s"
----

Required passport blocks: `metadata`, `topologyModes`, `workload`, `interfaces`, `hardwareAndKernel`, `operations`. Optional: `parameters`, `macros`, `scripts`. Each block is detailed in Part II and fully described in the reference.

=== From description to deploy

Compilation (`ami compile`) is a deterministic projection “1 card → 5–8 manifests”. The result is a GitOps cluster hierarchy of seven ordered stages with acyclic `dependsOn`:

[source,text]
----
clusters/<cluster-id>/
├── flux-system/                     # 0. GitOps control plane
├── 01-node-tuning/                  # 1. Kernel tuning profiles + Readiness Gate
├── 02-operators-and-networking/     # 2. Operators and load balancers
├── 03-stateful-clusters/            # 3. Datastore clusters
├── 04a-provisioners-day2/           # 4a. Idempotent DDL Jobs (-<sha8>)
├── 04b-app-migrations-expand/       # 4b. Schema expand migrations BEFORE rollout
├── 05-applications/                 # 5. Applications, HPA, KEP-753 sidecars
└── 06-zero-trust-and-observability/ # 6. NetworkPolicy, metrics, dashboards
----

Stage order prevents classic deadlocks: core and networking first, then data, then provisioning and migrations, and only then applications.

Compilation is reversible. Provenance labels (`amispec.io/component-id`, `amispec.io/passport`, `amispec.io/injected-sidecars`, `amispec.io/user-spec-delta`) are written into manifests so `ami decompile` can reconstruct the topology losslessly. For foreign (brownfield) clusters, `ami import-k8s` provides heuristic import with private-passport synthesis.

==== Capacity solver

Before a Git commit, the relationship graph is checked against the capacity model (node budgets and reservations):

* node RAM budget minus system reserve and slab;
* peak envelope under `maxReplicas` and `RollingUpdate maxSurge`;
* disk exclusivity (`diskExclusive`) and `mergeStrategy: exclusive-match` conflicts;
* forbid shrinking a volume below the already allocated size (`committedSizeGb`);
* CPU architecture and failure-domain compatibility;
* change impact preview (`Apply Impact Preview`): `in-place` / `hot-reload` / `rolling-switchover` / `pod-restart`.

Validation uses the same CEL logic as the compiler, so a consistent capacity model means a compilable topology.

=== The component passport in practice

The passport is reviewed block by block — from metadata to operations. Wherever a parameter protects against a specific failure, the reason is stated explicitly: that is the key to sound review.

==== Metadata, distribution, and visualization

[source,yaml]
----
metadata:
  id: postgresql                      # ^[a-z0-9-]+$
  name: "PostgreSQL HA"
  version: "18.0.0"                   # SemVer software version
  passportRev: 6                      # passport revision (>=1)
  category: database                  # database | analytics | cache | message-queue |
                                      # application | ai-ml | gateway | observability |
                                      # storage | security | external
  archetype: operator-cluster         # stateless-app | stateful-service | operator-cluster |
                                      # edge-gateway | external-bridge | scheduled-job
  distribution:
    tier: open-core                   # open-core | private-custom
    license: Apache-2.0
    vendor:                           # optional; required for commercial software
      name: "Example Vendor"
      requiresLicenseKey: true        # requests a sops license secret
  canvasUI:
    icon: "postgres-elephant"
    accentColor: "#336791"
    nodeShape: database-cylinder      # standard-card | database-cylinder | queue-pipe |
                                      # gateway-shield | external-cloud
    badges: ["HA", "R/W Split"]
    cardPinnedFields: ["topology.mode", "resources.memoryMb"]
    quickControls:
      - { field: "storage.sizeGb", label: "NVMe size", control: slider }
----

*Why `passportRev` is separate from `version`:* software version follows upstream; passport revision is the evolution of *our* rules (formulas, sidecars, quotas). `topology.yaml` pins `passportRev` so catalog updates do not silently recompile existing clusters.

*Why `distribution.tier`:* the open-core / private-custom boundary lets part of the catalog be community-facing while private passports live in `.amispec/private-specs/` without forking the schema.

==== User parameters (`parameters`)

The `parameters` dictionary is a declarative settings contract. It simultaneously drives tooling (forms, editors, APIs), validates `config` in the topology, and injects values into env or manifest `targetPath` — without raw Helm YAML.

[source,yaml]
----
parameters:
  log_min_duration_statement:
    label: "Slow-query log threshold (ms)"
    type: int                           # string | int | number | bool | enum | string-list | secret-ref
    default: 250
    min: 0
    max: 60000
    targetPath: "spec.postgresql.parameters.log_min_duration_statement"
    applyImpact: hot-reload             # pod-restart | hot-reload | rolling-switchover | immutable
    uiControl: number                   # input | number | slider | select | toggle | tags
  data_durability:
    label: "Synchronous replication guarantee"
    type: enum
    enumValues: ["async-fast", "sync-preferred", "sync-strict"]
    default: "async-fast"
    targetPath: "spec.postgresql.synchronous.dataDurability"
    applyImpact: hot-reload
    uiControl: select

----

If `targetPath` is omitted, the value is exported as a container environment variable. `applyImpact` is required: tooling can warn in advance whether a change needs a restart.

==== Macros and scripts

Two distinct extension circuits — deliberately not mixed.

[cols="2,3,3", options="header"]
|===
| | `macros` (compile-time) | `scripts` (runtime)

| *What it is*
| Pure CEL functions | Named executable scripts (`posix-sh`, `sql`, `python`)
| *When they run*
| At compile / solver time | In-cluster, inside Job / lifecycle / Day-2 actions
| *Limits*
| No I/O, terminate in < 1 ms | Never executed on the compiler host
| *How they are addressed*
| `passport.macros.<name>(...)`, `topology://macros/<name>` | `passport://scripts/<name>`, `topology://scripts/<name>`
| *How they are delivered*
| Inlined into computations | `ConfigMap`/`Secret` with `-<sha8>` suffix, mounted read-only
|===

[source,yaml]
----
macros:
  pgSharedBuffers: "string(int(memMb * 0.25)) + 'MB'"
scripts:
  seed-quotas:
    runtime: sql
    path: scripts/seed-tenant-quotas.sql
    timeoutSeconds: 300
    mountAsSecret: false
  verify-ledger:
    runtime: posix-sh
    inline: |
      set -euo pipefail
      test "$(wc -l < /var/run/ledger.pending)" -eq 0
----

Why scripts do not run at compile time: arbitrary code at build time breaks GitOps determinism, opens RCE via PRs, and kills solver latency. Therefore the compile-time circuit is pure CEL only; everything executable lives in the cluster with content-addressed naming: editing a script changes the `-<sha8>` hash and automatically restarts the Job.

==== Topology modes, quorums, and disruption budget

[source,yaml]
----
topologyModes:
  degraded-ha:
    description: "2 nodes Primary + Standby with Split-Brain protection"
    minNodes: 2
    replicas: 2
    maxReplicas: 2                      # e.g. 1 for singleton cron jobs
    scaleDownPolicy: safe-drain-required  # safe-drain-required | immediate
    quorumType: primary-standby         # none | raft-odd | primary-standby | witness-assisted
    quorumArbiter: cloud-lease-fencing  # none | witness-pod | cloud-lease-fencing
    witnessComponent:                   # lightweight quorum arbiter
      enabled: true
      replicas: 1
      image: "example/keeper:1.0"
      cpuMillicores: 100
      memoryMb: 192
      placement: control-plane-or-third-fault-domain
    disruptionBudget:
      maxUnavailable: 1
      updateStrategy: primary-last      # rolling | primary-last | recreate
    podAntiAffinity: hard-per-node      # soft | spread-skew-1 | hard-per-node | hard-per-zone | hard-per-disk
    uiWarning: "2-node mode: survives loss of 1 server."
----

*Why `quorumArbiter` in `degraded-ha`:* on two servers, a network split without a third quorum point leads either to a halt or to two primaries. A lightweight `witnessComponent` (hundreds of MB) or external `cloud-lease-fencing` blocks writes on the isolated node.

*Why `scaleDownPolicy: safe-drain-required`:* when shrinking a sharded cluster, stock Kubernetes simply kills the pod, leaving an orphaned PVC and losing un-drained partitions. The policy requires `preScaleDownDrainCommand` before removal.

*Why `podAntiAffinity: spread-skew-1`:* hard `hard-per-node` with `replicas > nodes` or `maxSurge: 1` leaves a pod in `Pending` forever. `spread-skew-1` maps to `topologySpreadConstraints` with `maxSkew: 1` and guarantees even spread without deadlock.

*Why `witnessComponent.replicas` and `cpuMillicores`:* `degraded-ha` needs one ~192 MB witness, while `strict-ha`/`sharded-cluster` deploy a full ring of three coordinators — without explicit fields capacity accounting would miss nearly a gigabyte of RAM.

workload:

    image: "registry.example.com/app:1.2.3"

*Why `imageDigest`:* floating tags cause version skew between replicas.

*Why `peerDiscovery: headless-early-dns`:* on cold start of a clustered StatefulSet nodes look each other up via DNS, but Kubernetes publishes DNS only after `readinessProbe`, while readiness waits for the quorum — an eternal deadlock. A Headless Service with `publishNotReadyAddresses: true` breaks the cycle.

*Why `readOnlyRootFilesystem`:* enables an auto-scratch tmpfs on `/tmp` and `/var/run` and protects against a log bomb in the rootfs.

==== Companions, probes, lifecycle

[source,yaml]
----
  companions:                          # tightly coupled companion containers in the same pod
    - name: nginx
      image: "nginx:1.27"
      imageDigest: "sha256:…"
      ports: [8080]
      memoryMb: 64
      cpuCores: 0.1
      mountPaths: ["/var/run/php-fpm"]
  probes:
    startup:
      type: http
      scheme: HTTPS
      path: "/health/startup"
      port: 8443
      failureThreshold: 30
      periodSeconds: 2
    liveness:
      type: http
      scheme: HTTPS
      path: "/health/live"
      port: 8443
      localProcessOnly: true            # do NOT call the database!
    readiness:
      type: tcp
      port: 8443
      checkLocalSidecars: true
      periodSeconds: 5
  lifecycle:
    preDeployExpandCommand: ["/app/cli", "db-migrate", "--phase=expand"]
    postDeployContractCommand: ["/app/cli", "db-migrate", "--phase=contract"]
    preScaleDownDrainCommand:
      scriptRef: "topology://scripts/drain-shard"
      args: ["--shard=${ordinal}"]
    jobTimeoutSec: 600
    jobBackoffLimit: 3
    rollingUpdateSurgePolicy: adaptive-zero-surge-on-tight-ram
    preStopSleepSec: 5
    gracefulStopTimeoutSec: 35
----

*Why the three probes are separate:* if `livenessProbe` checks database connectivity, a two-second primary switch will kill all backend pods. `liveness` is local process only; `readiness` may check sidecars.

*Why Expand/Contract in two Jobs:* migrations in every replica `entrypoint` race and deadlock; a destructive migration in `preDeploy` breaks old pods during `RollingUpdate`. `preDeployExpandCommand` runs before rollout, `postDeployContractCommand` after.

*Why `jobTimeoutSec` and the `-<sha8>` suffix:* a Kubernetes `Job` template is immutable; without a hash in the name Flux cannot update the Job. The hash solves both `field is immutable` and idempotency.

*Invariant `terminationGracePeriodSeconds`:* the compiler computes it as
`max(gracefulStopTimeoutSec, preStopSleepSec + max(maxEndpointTimeoutSec, maxStatementTimeoutSec))`
so no active stream or SQL statement is killed by `SIGKILL` before its legitimate timeout.

*Why `adaptive-zero-surge-on-tight-ram`:* with `maxSurge: 1` and only 400 MB free, the deploy hangs in `Pending`. If there is no headroom, the compiler switches the strategy to `maxSurge: 0, maxUnavailable: 1`.

==== `provides` slots and the client sidecar (`clientContract`)

This is the central mechanism of the standard (invariant #3). The database passport itself decides which local proxy to inject into the consumer pod.

[source,yaml]
----

    - slot: sql-cluster

      dialect: postgresql

        quotas:
          roleConnectionQuotaPercent: 50
        parameters:
          defaultStatementTimeoutSec: 60
    - slot: cdc-replication

          "spec.postgresql.parameters.wal_level": "logical"
          "spec.postgresql.parameters.max_replication_slots": "20"
      provisioner:
        driver: sql-job
        image: "postgres:18-alpine"
        template: "provisioners/init-cdc.sql.j2"

Key design decisions:

[cols="2,5", options="header"]
|===
| Mechanism | Failure it prevents

| `injectionTarget: k8s-native-init-restart-always`
| A plain sidecar starts in parallel with the app (`Connection refused` in the first ms), dies before the app, and blocks K8s Jobs. A native sidecar (KEP-753, `initContainers` + `restartPolicy: Always`) starts *before* the app, stops *after*, and terminates in Jobs.

| `deduplicationScope: per-target-component`
| Two sidecars for two databases of one cluster waste RAM; two different clusters break the port. Scope merges connections to one physical cluster into one sidecar.

| `portAllocationStrategy: deterministic-offset`
| `127.0.0.1:5432` collision with two Postgres clusters. The second cluster gets a deterministic offset (`15432`) and `${allocatedPorts.rw}` receives the actual port.

| `sharedQuotaFormula` + `graph.maxBoundConsumerPods`
| The pool is sized for the *peak*: `max(2, floor(0.80 * max_connections / maxBoundConsumerPods))`, where `maxBoundConsumerPods` = Σ (`coalesce(autoscaling.maxReplicas, replicas) + maxSurge`) of all consumers. The sum of pools never exceeds 80% of `max_connections`, even at HPA peak and RollingUpdate.

| `settings.maxPreparedStatements`
| Named prepared statements (ORM: Prisma, pgx, asyncpg, Django) conflict with `pool_mode = transaction`. Native protocol support allows transaction pooling without code changes.

| `listeners` rw/ro
| One port sends everything to the Primary, leaving Standby idle. Two listeners (`5432` Primary, `5433` round-robin over Standby with fallback) load read replicas.

| `preStopDrainDelaySec` only for `standard-container`
| With KEP-753 kubelet guarantees shutdown order: an extra `preStop: sleep 5` in every sidecar burns `terminationGracePeriodSeconds`.

| `provisioner.quotas` / `parameters`
| Noisy neighbor on a shared datastore: SQL role quotas and `defaultStatementTimeoutSec` isolate tenants.

| `onConsumerBindMutations`
| A CDC binding must not require manual `wal_level = logical` — the mutation applies automatically when the edge is drawn.
|===

==== `requires` requirements

[source,yaml]
----
  requires:
    database:
      capability: postgres-sql

      dependencyStage: infrastructure-ready

==== Hardware, storage, and kernel (`hardwareAndKernel`)

==== Resources

[source,yaml]
----
hardwareAndKernel:
  resources:
    supportedArchitectures: ["amd64", "arm64"]
    minCpuCores: 2.0
    minMemoryMb: 2048
    recommendedMemoryMb: 16384
    ephemeralStorageMb: 2048
    shmSizeMb: 512                      # /dev/shm; K8s default is 64 MB
    oomSafetyMarginPercent: 15          # 5..50
    numaTopologyPolicy: single-numa-node   # best-effort | single-numa-node
    networkQosClass: throughput-data    # realtime-interactive | throughput-data | background-bulk
    cpuLimitPolicy: burstable-no-limit  # burstable-no-limit | hard-cfs-limit
    verticalScalePolicy:
      cpu: in-place                     # in-place | pod-restart
      memory: in-place-grow-only        # in-place-grow-only | pod-restart
    accelerators:
      - { resourceClass: "nvidia.com/gpu", count: 1, minMemoryGb: 24 }
    cpuPinning: shared                  # shared | exclusive-static
----

*Why `shmSizeMb`:* Kubernetes defaults `/dev/shm` to 64 MB. PostgreSQL, NCCL tensor parallelism (PyTorch/vLLM), and Headless Chromium crash with `SIGBUS`. An explicit size mounts `emptyDir: Memory` and is subtracted from the node budget.

*Why `cpuLimitPolicy: burstable-no-limit`:* hard `limits.cpu == requests.cpu` enables Linux CFS quota, which freezes threads for 100 ms on micro-bursts even when the server is 80% idle. For memory, `requests.memory == limits.memory` is always required (OOM-Killer protection). The policy yields Guaranteed Memory + Burstable CPU.

*Why `verticalScalePolicy`:* KEP-1287 can change CPU and grow RAM in a live cgroup v2 without restart. In-place RAM shrink would OOM — it is done only via `pod-restart`/rolling-switchover.

*Why `networkQosClass`:* a nightly backup must not saturate the live traffic path; the class drives the eBPF EDT shaper (+ BBR).

==== Volumes (`storage` / `volumes`)

[source,yaml]
----
  storage:                             # single-volume shorthand (95% of cases)
    mountPath: "/var/lib/postgresql/data"
    minSizeGb: 10
    defaultSizeGb: 50
    resizePolicy: expand-only-monotonic   # expand-only-monotonic | immutable
    deletionPolicy: retain                # retain | delete
    nodeLossRecovery: rehydrate-from-backup
                                         # rehydrate-from-backup | reseed-from-peer | manual-wait
    mediaTier: nvme-local                 # nvme-local | ssd-network | hdd-bulk | ram-tmpfs
    diskExclusive: false
    lvmOptions:
      provisioner: topolvm-nvme           # topolvm-nvme | local-path | external-csi
      volumeBindingMode: WaitForFirstConsumer
      mountFlags: ["noatime", "nodiratime"]
      filesystem: xfs                     # xfs | ext4
      emergencyBallastMb: 1024
  volumes:                             # multi-volume configuration
    wal:  { mountPath: "/var/lib/pg/wal",  minSizeGb: 20, defaultSizeGb: 50, mediaTier: nvme-local, resizePolicy: expand-only-monotonic, deletionPolicy: retain, nodeLossRecovery: rehydrate-from-backup, lvmOptions: { provisioner: topolvm-nvme, volumeBindingMode: WaitForFirstConsumer, mountFlags: ["noatime"], filesystem: xfs, emergencyBallastMb: 1024 } }
    data: { mountPath: "/var/lib/pg/data", minSizeGb: 50, defaultSizeGb: 200, mediaTier: hdd-bulk,  resizePolicy: expand-only-monotonic, deletionPolicy: retain, nodeLossRecovery: rehydrate-from-backup, lvmOptions: { provisioner: topolvm-nvme, volumeBindingMode: WaitForFirstConsumer, mountFlags: ["noatime"], filesystem: xfs, emergencyBallastMb: 1024 } }
----

*Why `resizePolicy: expand-only-monotonic`:* XFS/ext4 and PVCs can only grow. The ratchet blocks shrinking the allocated size below `committedSizeGb` (including in interactive editors).

*Why `deletionPolicy: retain`:* GitOps `prune: true` on component rename deletes the PVC with production data. `retain` sets a protective annotation.

*Why `emergencyBallastMb`:* if a CDC reader stalls, WAL fills 100% of the disk, the datastore `PANIC`s and will not start. A 1–2 GB ballast is released via a service action so the database can boot.

*Why `mountFlags` is `#MountFlag` (closed set):* free-form mount option strings only fail at mount time; typos and dangerous flags (`suid`, `exec`, `dev`, `user`) are rejected at validation. The closed set covers common Linux/CSI options; defaults remain `["noatime", "nodiratime"]`.

*Why `volumeBindingMode: WaitForFirstConsumer`:* a TopoLVM volume lives on a specific node; without gravity binding the scheduler starts the pod where the volume is absent.

*Why `nodeLossRecovery`:* if a node dies physically, a standalone service without a recovery policy is dead forever. `rehydrate-from-backup` provisions a clean volume and restores a snapshot.

*Why `diskExclusive`:* Redpanda/ClickHouse need exclusive NVMe IOPS and cannot share one physical disk.

==== Kernel (`kernelTuning`) and constraints

[source,yaml]
----
  kernelTuning:
    priority: 80                       # 1..1000, profile merge priority
    maxClockSkewMs: 50
    kernelModules: ["wireguard", "nvme_tcp"]
    sysctl:
      vm.max_map_count: { value: 262144, mergeStrategy: max }

*Why `mergeStrategy`:* if ClickHouse leaves a node with `vm.max_map_count = 1600000`, resetting to default would kill remaining Elasticsearch. The daemon recomputes parameters from *remaining* pods; `exclusive-match` conflicts (e.g. THP modes) forbid co-location via `nodeAffinity`.

*Why `maxClockSkewMs`:* clock skew > 500 ms breaks Raft quorums, transactions, and JWT. Node readiness is affirmed only with a synchronized chronyd.

*Why `kernelModules`:* before setting the Readiness Gate the agent checks/loads allowlisted modules (`modprobe`).

*Why `prefer-standby`:* a heavy backup from the Primary degrades production transaction latency.

*Why `versionCoupling`:* PostgreSQL 18 + `pg_dump 16` produces corrupt archives; the backup tool must match the engine major version.

*Why `verifyRestoreJob`:* an ephemeral readability check of the latest backup catches “write-only backup”.

*Why `retentionDays`:* without retention, WAL and snapshots grow S3 without bound.

*Why `maxUploadBandwidthMbps`:* a 200 GB nightly upload must not saturate live user traffic.

In a topology a component can be declaratively initialized from a backup:

[source,yaml]
----
        bootstrap:
          fromBackupTarget: backups-s3
          sourceComponentId: pg-main
          recoveryTargetTime: "2026-10-19T14:30:00Z"   # or latest
----

==== Migration and cutover

[source,yaml]
----
  liveMigration:
    supportsExternalReplicaBootstrap: true
    supportedSources: ["aws-rds-postgres", "generic-postgres"]
    cutoverWorkflow:
      maxAllowedLagMs: 100
      steps: ["verify-lag-zero", "fence-source-ro", "promote-target-primary", "switch-edge-traffic"]
      actionId: "promote-external-replica-cutover"
----

Four steps prevent loss of the latest transactions: verify zero lag → fence source → promote target → switch traffic.

==== Day-2 actions

[source,yaml]
----
  day2Actions:
    - id: reload-pools
      title: "Reload connection pools"
      category: first-aid              # first-aid | maintenance | migration-cutover | scaling
      requiresConfirmation: true
      cooldownSeconds: 60
      ttlSecondsAfterFinished: 3600
      executionType: k8s-job           # gitops-patch | k8s-job | operator-annotation
      commandOrPatch: "kill -HUP 1"
      scriptRef: "passport://scripts/reload-pools"
----

*Why the `actionId` allowlist:* the control plane must not send arbitrary shell commands. The control plane commits a signed request `{actionId, componentId, triggerNonce}`; the agent matches `actionId` against the locally pinned passport, checks the nonce, and executes only the built-in `commandOrPatch`.

*Why `cooldownSeconds` and `ttlSecondsAfterFinished`:* double-clicking a button creates a job storm; finished Jobs without TTL clutter etcd.

*Why `dedupWindowHours`:* a continuous error stream is noisy and costly; the same error signature within 6 hours is processed once.

==== Cluster: security and networking

[source,yaml]
----
clusters:
  - id: prod-eu-central
    name: "Production EU-Central"
    domain: "example.io"
    k3sChannel: "v1.31"
    fleetCount: 10                     # for a fleet of homogeneous edge clusters
    maintenanceWindow: "Sun 02:00-05:00 UTC"
    security:
      sopsRecipients:                  # public Age keys of ALL controllers + owner key
        - "age1examplekey1…"
        - "age1examplekey2…"
      implicitTelemetryLayer: true
      imageRegistryPolicy: local-p2p-cache
                                       # local-p2p-cache | direct | airgapped-mirror
      registryMirrorEndpoint: "https://mirror.example.com"
      imagePullSecretRefs:
        - "sops://secrets/registry-pull.enc.yaml"
      controlPlaneQuorumMode: odd-etcd # odd-etcd | witness-arbiter
      etcdBackupTarget: backups-s3
    cni:
      engine: cilium-ebpf
      clusterMeshId: 1                 # 1..50
      kubeProxyReplacement: true
      hubbleLiveMap: true
      wireguardEncryption: true
      mtuDiscoveryAndMssClamp: true
      fqdnCacheMinTtlSec: 3600
----

*Why the `sopsRecipients` array:* if the first node dies, the second controller cannot decrypt Git without its key. Encryption targets all recipients at once.

*Why `imageRegistryPolicy: local-p2p-cache`:* during a 20-pod failover or in an air-gapped enclave, concurrent pulls hit Docker Hub limits (429) or fail offline. A P2P cache (Spegel) shares layers over the LAN.

*Why `clusterMeshId` and the CIDR formula:* IDs 1..50 deterministically allocate `Pod CIDR = 10.<100+id>.0.0/16` and `Service CIDR = 10.<200+id>.0.0/16` — non-overlapping across 50 clusters and not conflicting with the K3s default Service CIDR `10.43.0.0/16`.

*Why `fqdnCacheMinTtlSec: 3600`:* clouds return DNS TTL 5–60 s while keep-alive pools live for tens of minutes; without a 1-hour floor Cilium would cut live connections.

*Why `mtuDiscoveryAndMssClamp`:* WireGuard headers take 60–80 bytes from MTU; without TCP MSS clamping large packets (TLS handshake, INSERT batches) fall into an MTU black hole.

*Why `controlPlaneQuorumMode: witness-arbiter`:* on a 2-node cluster two-node etcd has quorum 2 — losing either server stops the API Server. The mode adds a witness or keeps the second node `worker-only`.

*Why `etcdBackupTarget`:* without embedded etcd snapshots, disk loss means manual volume rebinding and secret re-issuance.

[source,yaml]
----
    nodes:
      - id: node-bm-01
        role: control-plane-worker      # control-plane-worker | control-plane-only | worker-only
        pricingTier: standard-node      # micro-vps | standard-node | heavy-bare-metal

          arch: amd64                   # amd64 | arm64
          cpuCores: 16
          memoryMb: 65536
          systemReservedMb: 1280        # system RAM tax

          systemDiskReservedGb: 40      # OS, logs, image cache
          lvmSnapshotReservePercent: 10 # VG extent reserve for CoW snapshots
          accelerators:
            - { resourceClass: "nvidia.com/gpu", count: 1, memoryGb: 24 }

*Why reserves are explicit:* without `systemReservedMb` tools would treat all RAM as pod-available — the node OOMs. Without `systemDiskReservedGb` (30–50 GB for OS/cache) kubelet sets `DiskPressure` and evicts pods. Without `lvmSnapshotReservePercent` `restic-lvm-snapshot` fails `Insufficient free space in volume group` and nightly backups silently die.

*Why `failureDomainZone`:* without a failure domain on the node, `podAntiAffinity: hard-per-zone` cannot be checked before deploy.

Disk budget invariant enforced from the capacity model:

[source,text]
----
sum(allocatedStorageGb) <= disk.sizeGb * (1 - lvmSnapshotReservePercent/100) - systemDiskReservedGb
----

=== Bindings and data flows

A binding is a typed graph edge. It can be declared centrally in `clusters[].bindings[]` or inline in `components[].bindings` (without `from` — it is derived from the file owner).

[source,yaml]
----
bindings:
  - from: django-core
    to: pg-main
    toSlot: sql-cluster
    asRequirement: database
    envPrefix: "CACHE_"                # CACHE_REDIS_URL instead of REDIS_URL
    envOverrides:
      DATABASE_URL: "${exported.uri}"
    secretRotationEpoch: 1             # incrementing starts password rotation
    type: service-binding              # service-binding | live-migration-replica | oidc-protection
    deliveryGuarantee: direct          # direct | transactional-outbox
    outboxSourceRequirement: database  # which SQL slot is the outbox source
----

*Why `envPrefix`:* with `multiBind` to two Valkey instances (`cache` and `broker`) the second edge would overwrite the first `REDIS_URL`. The `CACHE_` / `BROKER_` prefixes separate the variables.

*Why `envOverrides`:* different apps on one archetype expect different names (`DATABASE_URL` vs `POSTGRES_DSN`) — override avoids a new passport.

*Why `secretRotationEpoch`:* generating random passwords on every compile creates Git noise and cascading restarts. While the epoch is unchanged the secret is reused idempotently; incrementing it regenerates the password, runs `ALTER ROLE`, and smoothly reloads pools.

*Why `deliveryGuarantee: transactional-outbox`:* dual-write (DB + queue) loses events when the network fails between calls. Outbox guarantees delivery; `outboxSourceRequirement` names the SQL database holding the outbox table and a light `outbox-relay` sidecar drains it to the broker.

*Why `type: oidc-protection`:* an edge from an identity provider to a `scope: protected` service automatically registers an OIDC client and configures middleware.

==== Typical HA application binding set

[source,yaml]
----
      - { from: web,     to: pg-main,  toSlot: sql-cluster,   asRequirement: database, secretRotationEpoch: 1 }
      - { from: workers, to: pg-main,  toSlot: sql-cluster,   asRequirement: database, secretRotationEpoch: 1 }
      - { from: web,     to: ch,       toSlot: analytics-sql, asRequirement: analytics_db }
      - { from: web,     to: valkey,    toSlot: kv-store,      asRequirement: cache,  envPrefix: "CACHE_" }
      - { from: workers, to: valkey,    toSlot: kv-store,      asRequirement: broker, envPrefix: "CELERY_BROKER_" }
      - { from: web,     to: sqs,       toSlot: sqs-endpoint,  asRequirement: queue,
          deliveryGuarantee: transactional-outbox, outboxSourceRequirement: database }
----

Note: two consumers of one `pg-main` do not create two sidecars (`deduplicationScope: per-target-component`), while two different Valkey instances get distinct local ports (`deterministic-offset`).

| Mode | Nodes | Composition | Quorum | When to use

| 1+
| 1 replica
| none
| Sandbox, dev, non-critical services

| `degraded-ha`
| 2
| 2 replicas + witness/lease
| `witness-assisted` / fencing
| Two-server SMB clusters, DR site

| `strict-ha`
| 3+
| 3 replicas, 0 RPO
| `raft-odd` / `primary-standby`

| 4+

| `raft-odd`

==== Special case: synchronous replication on two nodes

Hard `synchronous_standby_names = 'ANY 1 (*)'` on `degraded-ha` (1 Primary + 1 Standby) **fully blocks writes** when the only replica is lost — a two-node cluster becomes less available than a single server.

Therefore the passport defines `data_durability`:

* `async-fast` — asynchronous replication, minimal latency;
* `sync-preferred` — synchronous while Standby is alive; if the replica dies the operator *self-heals* and relaxes the lock (safe for 2 nodes);
* `sync-strict` — requires a live synchronous replica (for `strict-ha` on 3+ nodes, RPO = 0).

=== Complex scenarios (real-world configurations)

Below are condensed scenario maps used to validate the standard. Each answers “which AmiSpec fields come into play”.

==== High-load SaaS / FinTech core

Stack: edge → web (4 replicas) + workers (HPA 2..8) + singleton beat → PostgreSQL HA + ClickHouse HA + Valkey HA + S3 backups.

Used: two native sidecars (`pgbouncer` + `chproxy`) in one pod; R/W split `5432/5433`; `sharedQuotaFormula` on HPA peak + maxSurge; `maxReplicas: 1` + `Recreate` for beat (else periodic jobs double-run); `zeroEndpoints: true` on the worker; `preDeployExpandCommand` Job; `deliveryGuarantee: transactional-outbox` for webhooks.

==== AdTech RTB and event pipeline

Stack: L4/BGP VIP → bidder (20 replicas, SLA < 10 ms) → Redpanda → consumer → ClickHouse Sharded + Valkey Cluster.

Used: `diskExclusive: true` separates Redpanda and ClickHouse onto physical NVMe; `volumes` map (hot NVMe / cold HDD); `vm.max_map_count = 1600000`, `fs.aio-max-nr`, `ioScheduler: none`; `numaTopologyPolicy: single-numa-node`; `cpuPinning: exclusive-static` for latency-critical paths.

==== Polyglot OLTP → OLAP with CDC

Stack: app → PostgreSQL (OLTP) → Debezium → bus → ClickHouse (OLAP) → BI.

Used: `onConsumerBindMutations` sets `wal_level: logical` and `max_replication_slots`; `max_slot_wal_keep_size` formula (15% of disk) + `emergencyBallastMb`; `maxUploadBandwidthMbps` on backups; `scope: protected` for BI.

==== Autonomous enclave and air-gapped registry

Stack: git + tracker + registry + CI + two independent Valkey instances.

Used: `deduplicationScope` + `deterministic-offset` (ports `6379` and `16379`); `imageRegistryPolicy: airgapped-mirror` / `local-p2p-cache`; `maxBodySizeMb: 0` for image-layer pushes; `egressDefaultMode: graph-only`.

==== SSO / Identity-Aware Proxy

Stack: Keycloak HA → identity database → L7 with OIDC filter in front of internal services.

Used: `provisioner.driver: oidc-client-job`; `type: oidc-protection` on bindings; `securityPolicy.clientAuth: oidc` on `expose`.

==== Low-latency market-data processing

Used: `cpuPinning: exclusive-static`, `numaTopologyPolicy: single-numa-node`, `networkQosClass: realtime-interactive`, `allowedFqdns` for exchange WebSockets, `egressDefaultMode: allow-fqdn-whitelist`.

==== Search / log JVM cluster

Used: heap formula `min(int(resources.memoryMb * 0.5), 31744)` (31 GB cap for compressed OOPs); `vm.max_map_count >= 262144` with `mergeStrategy: max`; `incompatiblePassportsOnSameNode` for disk monopoly.

==== Legacy modernization (Strangler Fig)

Stack: one domain → path routing (`/api/v2/*` → new service; `/*` → monolith).

Used: `expose[].pathPrefix`; `workload.companions` (nginx + php-fpm over a UNIX socket); `configMounts` for mounted configs (`php.ini`) from sops secrets; `sessionAffinity: cookie-sticky`.

==== Media streaming and object storage

Used: `minNodes: 4` for Erasure Coding (solver-checked before commit); `autoscaling.customMetric` on transcode queue length; scratch volume `mediaTier: nvme-local` for ffmpeg.

==== Banking enclave with hard zoning

Used: three `securityZones` (`dmz-public`, `app-core`, `restricted-vault`) with `podSecurityStandard: restricted`; `forbiddenConsumerZones: ["dmz-public"]` on the secret-store passport; `clientAuth: mtls`; `tlsMode: auto-internal-ca`.

==== Observability

Used: `implicitTelemetryLayer: true` (monitoring edges do not clutter the relationship graph); `localAnomalyRules` (Z-score, disk regression) + `logPolicy.redactPii`; `logPolicy.redactPii`.

==== Multi-cluster DR and geo-distribution

Used: `crossClusterLinks`; unique `clusterMeshId` values; `sopsRecipients` per cluster; `bootstrap.fromBackupTarget` for the DR instance; `rpoTargetSec` and `maxBandwidthMbps`.

==== Fleet Hub-and-Spoke

Used: `fleetCount: 10` on the edge cluster; `relationType: telemetry-and-events-uplink`; local `standalone` PostgreSQL at the store + a central analytics cluster.

=== CEL formulas, tokens, and macros

==== Evaluation context

CEL expressions in `configFormulas`, `sharedQuotaFormula`, `topologyModes[].replicas`, and `parameters[].visibleWhen` can use:

[cols="2,4", options="header"]
|===
| Expression | Meaning

| `resources.memoryMb`, `resources.cpuCores`
| Instance limits from `topology.yaml`

| `storage.sizeGb`
| Size of the primary volume

| `configFormulas.<name>`
| Value of an already computed formula (as in `sharedQuotaFormula`)

| `graph.maxBoundConsumerPods`
| Peak consumer budget: Σ(`coalesce(autoscaling.maxReplicas, replicas) + maxSurge`) of all bound components

| `topology.mode`, `topology.shards`, `topology.replicas`
| Instance mode parameters

| `service.rwDns`, `service.httpDns`, etc.
| Provider service DNS names

| `allocatedPorts.<listener>`
| Actual sidecar local port (after `deterministic-offset`)

| `credentials.username`, `.password`, `.database`
| Credentials created by the provisioner

| `exported.<token>` / `${exported.<key>}`
| `clientContract.exportedTokens` and external `exportedAttributes` tokens
|===

==== Token templates

`envMapping` and `exportedTokens` use `${...}` substitutions and computed `=${...}` strings:

[source,yaml]
----
exportedTokens:
  host: "=${ topology.mode == 'standalone' ? service.rwDns : '127.0.0.1' }"
  uri:  "postgresql://${credentials.username}:${credentials.password}@${exported.host}:${allocatedPorts.rw}/${credentials.database}"
requires:
  database:
    envMapping:
      DATABASE_URL: "${exported.uri}"
----

==== Typical formulas

[cols="2,3,3", options="header"]
|===
| Task | Formula | Comment

| PostgreSQL `shared_buffers`
| `string(int(resources.memoryMb * 0.25)) + 'MB'`
| 25% RAM; `rolling-switchover`

| PostgreSQL `work_mem`
| `string(max(4, int((resources.memoryMb * 0.20) / max(100, min(500, int(resources.memoryMb / 16)))))) + 'MB'`
| Tied to `max_connections`; peak avoids OOM

| PostgreSQL `max_slot_wal_keep_size`
| `string(int(storage.sizeGb * 1024 * 0.15)) + 'MB'`
| 15% of disk for WAL

| ClickHouse `max_server_memory_usage`
| `string(int(resources.memoryMb * 0.80) * 1048576)`
| 80% ceiling, `unit: bytes`

| JVM heap (OpenSearch etc.)
| `min(int(resources.memoryMb * 0.5), 31744)`
| 31 GB cap (compressed OOPs)

| PgBouncer pool quota
| `max(2, int((int(configFormulas.max_connections) * 0.80) / max(1, graph.maxBoundConsumerPods)))`
| Sum of pools ≤ 80% of `max_connections`

| Sharded cluster replicas
| `"=${ topology.shards * 2 }"`
| Computed `replicas` in the mode
|===

* *Service discovery plane* (DNS + NetworkPolicy): allows `A ↔ B` cycles.
* *Boot-order plane* (`dependsOn`): acyclic DAG only on `dependencyStage: infrastructure-ready`; `runtime-parallel` is one wave.

==== Core compiler invariants
[[compiler-invariants]]

Below are *normative* compilation rules. A conforming compiler implementation MUST enforce each of them.

. `terminationGracePeriodSeconds` MUST be computed as `max(gracefulStopTimeoutSec, preStopSleepSec + max(maxEndpointTimeoutSec, maxStatementTimeoutSec))`.
. The sum of connection pools MUST stay ≤ 80% of `max_connections` even at HPA `maxReplicas` + `maxSurge`.
. Shrinking `storage.sizeGb` / `replicas` below `committed*` MUST be blocked.
. Formulas with `applyImpact: rolling-switchover` MUST be deferred to `maintenanceWindow` on in-place RAM growth; on RAM shrink, formulas MUST apply first, then cgroup shrink.
. Migration and provisioning Jobs MUST get the `-<sha8>` suffix and `ttlSecondsAfterFinished`.
. Pod CIDR MUST be computed as `10.<100+clusterMeshId>.0.0/16`, Service CIDR as `10.<200+clusterMeshId>.0.0/16`.
. `preStopDrainDelaySec` MUST be added only when `injectionTarget: standard-container`.
. Runtime `scripts` MUST NOT execute on the compiler host; they MUST be packaged content-addressed (`-<sha8>`) and mounted read-only.
. Provenance labels (see <<provenance>>) MUST be present in generated manifests.
. Compilation MUST be deterministic: identical (passports + topology) yield the same manifest tree (up to allowed timestamp fields).

=== Reverse compilation (decompile)
[[reverse-compilation]]

Reverse compilation reconstructs AmiSpec configuration documents from a
generated (or provenance-annotated) Kubernetes / FluxCD manifest tree. It is
the counterpart of <<_compilation_model>>: forward compile expands one graph
node into many manifests; reverse compile folds those manifests back into
topology and (where declared) passport references.

==== Fidelity levels

[cols="2,4,4", options="header"]
|===
| Level | Input | Guarantee

| *A. Lossless decompile*
| Tree produced by a conforming compiler, carrying Provenance labels (<<provenance>>)
| Round-trip is bijective on user-level values: reconstructed `TopologyBlueprint` equals the source instance for all fields encoded in `user-spec-delta`, labels and annotations.

| *B. Heuristic import* (`import-k8s` / brownfield)
| Foreign K8s / Flux / Helm manifests without AmiSpec Provenance
| Best-effort classification and synthesis. Output is a valid AmiSpec topology plus private passports; it is *not* required to be bit-identical to any historical source.
|===

This document specifies *Level A* as normative. Level B is a product workflow:
its detection heuristics are informative; only the *output contract* (valid
AmiSpec documents) is normative.

==== Input contract (normative)

A conforming lossless decompiler MUST accept any tree that a conforming
compiler can emit and MUST treat the following labels / annotations as
authoritative (see <<provenance>>):

[cols="2,4", options="header"]
|===
| Key | Reconstructs

| `amispec.io/component-id`
| `components[].id`

| `amispec.io/passport`
| `components[].passport`, `version`, `passportRev` via `id@version-rev`

| `amispec.io/zone`
| `components[].zone` (and namespace of the workload)

| `amispec.io/cluster-id`
| `clusters[].id`

| `amispec.io/injected-sidecars`
| Sidecar set (filtered from `initContainers`; not re-emitted as user config)

| `amispec.io/binding-edge`
| `bindings[]` edges in `from->to:slot` form

| `amispec.io/user-spec-delta`
| JSON delta of `topology`, `resources`, `config`, `group`, `backupTarget`, `zone`

| `amispec.io/stage`
| Stage placement (diagnostic only; not required to round-trip as user config)

| `amispec.io/cluster-id` (workloads)
| Cluster membership when path inference is unavailable
|===

If an object carries no AmiSpec Provenance, the decompiler MUST NOT invent a
component identity for it; it MAY skip the object and report a warning.

==== Reconstruction mapping

[cols="2,3,4", options="header"]
|===
| Manifest source | Topology field | Notes

| `NodeTuningProfile` metadata/labels + `spec.hardware`
| `clusters[].nodes[]`
| `id` from `amispec.io/node-id` (or name suffix); CPU/RAM/disk/reserves from `spec.hardware`; `failureDomainZone` from `spec`

| `GatewayClass`
| `clusters[].loadBalancers[]`
| `id`, `layer`, `provider`, `tls`, `forwardingMode` from labels/annotations

| `Deployment` / `StatefulSet`
| `clusters[].components[]`
| Passport pin, `replicas`, `zone`/`namespace`, `resources` from container requests, delta overlay

| `Secret` (`*-bindings`)
| `bindings[]`, `user-spec-delta`
| `amispec.io/binding-edge` → edges; delta restores `config` / `backupTarget`

| `HTTPRoute`
| `components[].expose[]`
| `endpoint`, `viaBalancer`, `scope`, `hostname`, `pathPrefix`

| `PodDisruptionBudget`
| implicit (mode/disruption)
| Not stored as user config; presence informs default `disruptionBudget`

| `HorizontalPodAutoscaler`
| `components[].topology.autoscaling`
| `minReplicas`, `maxReplicas`, `cooldownSeconds` (stabilization window)

| `Job` (`provision-*`, `migrate-expand-*`)
| lifecycle hooks
| Job names are content-addressed; hooks are not required to round-trip as command text

| `CiliumNetworkPolicy`
| egress posture
| `graph-only` default implied; not user-editable topology data

| `ConfigMap` `_meta/*`
| `externalResources[]`, `crossClusterLinks[]`
| Role, endpoint, transport, relation type, RPO / bandwidth
|===

==== Round-trip guarantees (normative)

For a tree produced by a conforming compiler from topology instance `T`:

. `decompile(compile(T))` MUST yield a topology `T'` such that:
  * `T'.clusters[].id`, `components[].id`, `passport`, `version`, `passportRev` are equal to `T`;
  * `components[].topology.mode`, `replicas`, `committedReplicas` and `resources` are equal to `T` (from `user-spec-delta`);
  * `components[].zone`, `group`, `backupTarget`, `config` are equal to `T` when present;
  * cluster-level `bindings[]` derived from provenance edges match `T`'s edges (`to`, `toSlot`).
. Synthetic objects introduced only for compilation (KEP-753 sidecars, `tmp-scratch`, hashed Job names) MUST be omitted from `T'`.
. Fields not represented in manifests (for example `macros`, `scripts` bodies) MUST NOT be invented; their absence in `T'` is not a round-trip violation.
. The decompiler MUST NOT execute runtime scripts or call external APIs.

Note: `compile(decompile(M))` for a *foreign* tree `M` is not required to be
byte-identical to `M`; only Level A (provenance-carrying trees) is bijective.

==== Modes

[cols="2,4,4", options="header"]
|===
| Mode | Pipeline | Normative output

| *Lossless decompile*
| Read tree → parse Provenance → reconstruct instances → emit `topology.yaml` (and optional `topology/` modules)
| Valid `TopologyBlueprint` (`topology.amispec.io/v1`) satisfying <<conformance>> topology rules

| *Heuristic import*
| Classify workloads (CRD / image / chart signatures) → map services & ports → recover bindings from env/secrets/netpols → synthesize private passports in `.amispec/private-specs/`
| Valid `ComponentPassport` + `TopologyBlueprint` pair; private passports use `distribution.tier: private-custom`
|===

==== Example (lossless)

.Input provenance fragment (`05-applications/workloads.yaml`)
[source,yaml]
----

  name: web
  labels:
    amispec.io/component-id: "web"
    amispec.io/passport: "stateless-app@1.0.0-rev1"

  annotations:
    amispec.io/user-spec-delta: '{"topology":{"mode":"standalone","replicas":2},"resources":{"cpuCores":1,"memoryMb":512}}'

.Reconstructed fragment (`topology.yaml`)
[source,yaml]

        version: "1.0.0"

----

==== Conformance for reverse compilation

A tool claims *AmiSpec reverse-compiler (Level A)* conformance when:

. it reconstructs a valid `TopologyBlueprint` from any tree this specification's compiler can emit;
. round-trip guarantees above hold for all fields listed in `user-spec-delta`;
. objects without Provenance are skipped with a warning, never guessed;
. no runtime script from the tree is executed during decompile.

=== Passport author checklist
[[checklist-passport]]

Checklist is *informative*; items are best practice (RFC 2119: SHOULD / RECOMMENDED) unless MUST is stated.

. `metadata.id` MUST match `^[a-z0-9-]+$`; `passportRev` SHOULD be incremented on any rule change.
. Document all supported `topologyModes` with honest `minNodes` and `uiWarning` for degraded modes.
. `configFormulas` cover all memory-dependent parameters; `applyImpact` is set.
. `livenessProbe` does NOT depend on external services.
. For HA datastores, `clientContract` is filled with `sharedQuotaFormula` and R/W `listeners`.
. `storage.deletionPolicy: retain` for stateful; `resizePolicy: expand-only-monotonic` is explicit.
. `kernelTuning.mergeStrategy` is deliberate: `max` for limits, `min` for aggressive values, `exclusive-match` for incompatible modes.
. `backup` includes `retentionDays`, `verifyRestoreJob`, `versionCoupling`.
. `day2Actions` use only built-in commands/scripts; `cooldownSeconds` and `ttlSecondsAfterFinished` are set.
. For commercial software: `distribution.tier: private-custom`, `requiresLicenseKey: true`, `licenseSecretRef` guidance.
. The passport has been run through capacity validation in the modes you claim.

=== Topology author checklist
[[checklist-topology]]

Checklist is *informative*; items are best practice (SHOULD) unless MUST is stated.

. `projectId` and `clusterMeshId` MUST be unique; the CIDR formula must not clash with existing networks.
. `sopsRecipients` contains keys of all controllers and the owner recovery key.
. Each node honestly lists `memoryMb`, disks, and reserves; SSD/NVMe set `systemDiskReservedGb` and `lvmSnapshotReservePercent`.
. Component `resources.memoryMb` fits the node budget including sidecars, witness, and `systemReservedMb`.
. Bindings use `envPrefix` under `multiBind`; `secretRotationEpoch` is explicit.
. `committedReplicas` / `committedSizeGb` are set for stateful components.
. For external migration: `externalResources` + `type: live-migration-replica` + `bootstrap` on the target.
. Multi-cluster: each cluster has its own `clusterMeshId`; `crossClusterLinks` carry `rpoTargetSec` and `maxBandwidthMbps`.
. `expose` covers only needed endpoints; `securityPolicy` is enabled for public APIs.
. Layout coordinates are not part of AmiSpec (UI auto-layout only).
